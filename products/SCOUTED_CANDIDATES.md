## ⭐ PRIMARY EVIDENCE (strongest data in this file)

**The operator is himself a buyer in this market.** He purchased restaurant-owner
subscriptions and *"ended up with excel sheets."*

This outranks every search result in the register, because it is first-hand rather
than inferred:

1. **Monetization: CONFIRMED.** Not deduced from listings — a real person in this
   exact market paid real money.
2. **Paid incumbents underdeliver.** He bought a solution and received documents.
   The problem survived the purchase.
3. **Therefore the market is not saturated with solutions** — it is saturated with
   *documents presenting themselves as solutions*, at both the free tier (Toast,
   Square lead magnets) and the paid tier (owner subscriptions).

**Consequence, and it kills our own plan:** a gamified document pack would deliver
the same disappointment he already paid for. Do not ship documents into a market
whose defining complaint is "I paid and got documents."

**The gap:** pain is universal, solutions are enterprise-priced, and the affordable
end of the market is static documents. The middle — an actual onboarding *system* an

**Open risk:** headroom — and a later search made it sharper, not softer.

**Free-substitute pressure (found by attacking this candidate deliberately):**
Free restaurant onboarding checklists are abundant and thorough — Toast POS, Coast,
FirstHR, Agendrix, Miratag, Qwick, RestaurantOwner. POS and scheduling companies give
these away as **lead magnets for their SaaS**, which means the free tier is funded by
someone else's business model and will never stop being free.

Re-scored both ways:

| headroom | reading | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | a gamified *system* is a different category from a checklist | **STRONG** |
| 2 | buyers see "a prettier checklist" | **KILLED** |

**The entire candidate turns on one judgement a search cannot settle.**
- *For:* paid Etsy handbooks sell perfectly well even though free handbooks exist —
  people pay for design, editability and not having to assemble it themselves.
- *Against:* the free checklists are genuinely comprehensive and backed by Toast/Square.

Needs the operator's read, or a cheap test, before any build.

## ⚠️ Structural lesson: the lead-magnet floor

Two separate candidates died the same way, and it generalises:

**In any market where a funded SaaS sells the workflow, that SaaS gives the
*documents* away free to generate leads.** Square, Toast, 7shifts and Agendrix all
publish free restaurant templates — not as a product, but as bait for their
subscription. Their free tier is subsidised by a different business model, so it will
never get worse and never start charging.

**Implication for the scout:** in any vertical with well-funded SaaS incumbents,
score `monetization` and `headroom` DOWN for anything document- or template-shaped,
regardless of how real the underlying pain is. The pain being real is exactly why
someone else is already giving away the cure.

**Where this does NOT apply:** categories where buyers pay for *design and assembly*
rather than information — proven by paid Etsy handbooks selling alongside free ones.
The question is always whether the paid thing is a different *category* or just a
nicer version of the free thing.
