## Candidate Card A — Makeover Simulator

**Plain-language idea.** Preview a coordinated wardrobe, haircut/color, tattoo placement, and appearance concept on a consenting adult before expensive real-world changes.

**First buyer and painful job.** An adult planning a meaningful appearance change who is afraid that individually attractive choices will clash, fit poorly, or create expensive regret.

**Why the niche may pay.** Google’s own-photo try-on and YouCam’s broad appearance-editing suite validate the behavior. Google also explicitly warns that its experimental output may be inaccurate, leaving room for a narrower, honestly limited decision-support service rather than a “truthful future body” promise.

**Smallest sellable test.** A manually reviewed three-look concept pack using one depicted adult’s own consented photo, purchasable garment links, a style rationale, and explicit limitations: not a fit guarantee, body prediction, health claim, tattoo outcome, or professional styling advice. Intake must bind age and consent to the **depicted subject**, not merely the purchaser, and require same-person attestation. Source images and derivatives need a declared deletion schedule.

**Same-day derivative.** A no-photo transformation decision workbook/checklist at BHD 3–8. It is lower-risk but tests workbook demand, not visual-simulation demand.

**Revenue path.** Sell the manual pack at BHD 8–25; only after payment evidence consider premium simulations, affiliate links, or stylist/salon/tattoo referrals. Affiliate availability, vendor sizing accuracy, inventory continuity, and referral agreements remain unverified.

**Commercial test and kill rule.** Separate one-time setup (`12–25h`) from per-order fulfillment (`45–90m` target). After explicit live-test authorization, present a paid offer to 10 qualified adults. Continue only if at least two external payments settle **and** settled revenue covers direct cash plus a declared labor floor; kill or radically narrow after two changed offers if no payment settles or delivery remains above 90 minutes. Do not run the test with minors, third-party images, intimate images, or weight-change predictions.

**Strongest argument.** A vivid preview can reduce regret before purchases whose combined value greatly exceeds the test price, and it can connect directly to commerce.

**Strongest rejection argument.** Google and YouCam already cover much of the visual feature set; truthful coordination across clothes, hair, tattoos, and body appearance is difficult, while image/biometric privacy, bias, body-image harm, and compute/fulfillment economics create outsized downside.

## Candidate Card B — Autonomous TikTok Growth Operator

**Plain-language idea.** A governed engine that repeatedly tests original short-form brand theses for legitimate sponsor-owned accounts, measures attributable outcomes, scales winners, and retires weak hypotheses.

**First buyer and painful job.** Initially a small seller or creator who has something legitimate to sell but cannot sustain the trend research, scripting, production, posting, measurement, and iteration loop.

**Smallest sellable test.** A seven-day TikTok experiment pack: one niche thesis, audience contract, seven original scripts/hooks, captions, shot/edit briefs, disclosure notes, and a measurement sheet. The human owner approves and posts. Price hypothesis: BHD 25–75; setup/delivery assumption: 15–30 hours and BHD 0–40 cash.

**Same-day derivative.** A niche-specific hook/script/template pack at BHD 5–15. It tests low-support SKU demand, not autonomous operation.

**Revenue path.** Sell packs or a managed service first. Later paths may include the account owner’s products, disclosed affiliates/sponsorships, and platform-native rewards. TikTok Creator Rewards availability in Bahrain is **UNVERIFIED**, so it is excluded from the base case.

**Maximum compliant automation boundary.** Research, drafts, asset provenance, policy checks, experiment allocation, analytics normalization, and recommendations can be automated locally. Account creation/verification, ownership, consent/OAuth, app review, public-post scope, commercial disclosures, monetization enrollment, tax/payment setup, credentials, and deletion remain human/live gates. Public automation must use a current approved interface and exact sponsor scope.

**Portfolio guardrails.** Pilot one active TikTok brand. A retired brand needs a durable reason; a same-formula successor is blocked until the hypothesis materially changes. “Churn” applies to niches, formats, offers, creative hypotheses, and legitimate weak brands—not fake identities, bulk account creation, repeated content, verification/CAPTCHA bypass, ban evasion, or synthetic engagement.

**Commercial test and kill rule.** After live authorization, show two materially distinct versions of the paid pack to 10 qualified buyers. Kill the wedge if no external payment settles. Do not build a portfolio layer until one sponsor-controlled channel completes a probation window with original content, zero policy strikes, bounded production cost, and attributable settled external income.

**Strongest argument.** Short-form iteration creates rapid behavioral feedback, and the first revenue can come from a service/product buyer rather than waiting for platform payouts.

**Strongest rejection argument.** TikTok’s official rules directly constrain the strongest “autonomous portfolio” interpretation: realistic AIGC disclosure, original content, real identities, consented publishing, app audit, and anti-spam/anti-evasion protections make unattended account launch-and-churn commercially and technically unsafe.

## Candidate Card C — Autonomous YouTube Studio

**Plain-language idea.** A governed engine that repeatedly tests original YouTube brand theses, produces distinct videos, attributes commercial outcomes, scales winners, and retires weak hypotheses.

**First buyer and painful job.** Initially a niche expert or small business wanting evergreen video without owning the entire research, scripting, editing, packaging, and learning loop.

**Smallest sellable test.** One original research-to-video package—thesis, sourced script, voice/visual plan, edit, thumbnail/title options, metadata, disclosure decision, and analytics plan—with human approval and posting. Price hypothesis: BHD 40–150; one-time workflow setup/delivery assumption: 20–40 hours and BHD 0–75 cash.

**Same-day derivative.** A three-video launch blueprint at BHD 10–40. It tests strategy-package demand, not full production economics.

**Revenue path.** Sell production/blueprint work first, then attributable affiliate or owned-product revenue. Bahrain is eligible for expanded YPP, but thresholds and channel review apply; native ad revenue is therefore a later path, never assumed first revenue.

**Maximum compliant automation boundary.** Research, drafts, production assistance, asset provenance, safety/copyright checks, thumbnail/title experiments, analytics ingestion, and recommendations can be automated. Account creation, 2-step verification, OAuth, public publication scope, content ownership attestations, disclosure judgment for realistic synthetic media, YPP/AdSense, tax/payment, credentials, and channel deletion remain human/live gates.

**Portfolio guardrails.** Pilot one active YouTube channel and one format family. Content must be independently useful and materially original. Repetitive templates, low-value reused material, near-duplicate channel launches, and mass-produced videos are disqualifying even when technically AI-generated. Retire hypotheses with documented reasons; never rebrand the same failed formula to evade review.

**Commercial test and kill rule.** After live authorization, show two materially distinct package offers to 10 qualified buyers. Kill if no payment settles or if the third delivery still cannot meet the declared labor-margin floor. For an owned-channel pilot, demand attributable external settled income before portfolio scale; views, subscribers, compliments, and YPP eligibility alone do not pass.

**Strongest argument.** Bahrain YPP availability is verified, YouTube supports AI production assistance with disclosure boundaries, and an evergreen content library can support services, affiliates, owned products, and later ad revenue.

**Strongest rejection argument.** Eligibility is slow and reviewed, production quality is expensive, and YouTube explicitly rejects repetitive/mass-produced inauthentic content—the cheapest interpretation of “fully autonomous” is the one least likely to monetize.

## Recommended Blueprint Split for the Media Engines

### Commercial packaging

Treat TikTok and YouTube as **two independently sellable engines/modules** now because their first buyers, deliverables, cycle times, native metrics, and policy risks differ. Present a common architecture direction, not a mandatory shared build.

### Shared Media Portfolio Kernel — extraction gate

Extract a shared kernel only after each adapter’s one-channel wedge has at least one external settled payment and the team can name at least three repeated shared operations. Candidate shared components are: evidence ledger, brand thesis/audience contract, asset provenance, policy/safety checks, human identity gate, experiment registry, attribution ledger, lifecycle/stop-loss controller, and validated learning memory.

Lifecycle: `proposed → researched → account_gate → probation → active → scaling → paused → retired`.

The kernel must not own credentials or mint publishing authority. Its portfolio controller records hypotheses and evidence; each platform adapter enforces the narrower current platform boundary.

### TikTok adapter gates

1. Sponsor-owned account and human verification.
2. Current approved publishing interface, explicit consent, and app audit where required.
3. Originality, AIGC, IP, commercial-disclosure, spam, and identity checks.
4. Probation: one account, one offer, bounded content sample, zero strikes.
5. Scale: external settled income attributable to the channel plus acceptable production/support cost.
6. Pause/retire: stop-loss breach, policy warning, repeated zero traction, or negative contribution margin.

### YouTube adapter gates

1. Sponsor-owned channel, 2-step verification, and human ownership.
2. OAuth/audited publishing path and human public-release scope.
3. Originality/reused-content, copyright, disclosure, thumbnail/title, and safety checks.
4. Probation: one channel and one distinct format family.
5. Scale: attributable settled income and repeatable contribution margin; YPP eligibility alone is insufficient.
6. Pause/retire: policy/review risk, repeated zero traction, unsustainable production burden, or negative contribution margin.
